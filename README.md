# Thermal Processing and Mechanical Characterization of Sintered Clay Ceramics

**Author:** Daksh Garg (Entry No: 2023MMB1410)  
**Institution:** Indian Institute of Technology Ropar, Department of Metallurgical and Materials Engineering  
**Course:** Development Engineering Project (CP301)  

## Project Overview
This project investigates the processing-structure-property relationships of scaled-down traditional ceramic building materials. The study evaluates how varying thermal treatment parameters (firing temperatures and extended holding times) influence the densification, vitrification, and ultimate mechanical properties of clay-based ceramics.

## Experimental Methodology
*   **Sample Fabrication:** Designed and fabricated a custom 25-gauge Galvanized Iron (GI) sheet mold to produce 42 scaled-down ceramic test specimens (1/8th standard volume) to optimize resource and energy efficiency.
*   **Thermal Processing:** Conducted controlled firing in a laboratory muffle furnace at 800°C, 900°C, and 1000°C, utilizing variable thermal soaking durations of 9, 12, 18, 24, and 36 hours.
*   **Mechanical Testing:** Evaluated initial load-bearing capacity using a 3000 kN Compression Testing Machine (CTM) at a loading rate of 14 MPa/min.
*   **Physical Characterization:** Quantified thermal weight loss percentages and performed 24-hour water absorption testing in accordance with IS 3495 (Part 2) standards.

## Key Results & Metallurgical Findings
*   **Phase Transformations:** Identified that firing at 1000°C provided the necessary thermal energy for the partial melting of silica (vitrification) and the complete oxidation of iron compounds into hematite, sealing internal pores and creating a dense matrix.
*   **Mechanical Optimization:** Determined that a peak firing temperature of 1000°C with a 36-hour holding time yielded a maximum compressive strength of 11.83 MPa, successfully exceeding the 10.5 MPa threshold for premium First-Class structural materials.
*   **Porosity Control:** Achieved optimal water absorption rates between 13.72% and 14.99% for 900°C and 1000°C batches, falling perfectly within the 5% to 20% tolerance required for adequate mortar bonding without compromising durability.
*   **Defect Analysis:** Identified that large, uncrushed impurities (e.g., stones) induce severe internal stresses due to thermal expansion mismatches and uneven shrinkage, leading to catastrophic structural failure during compressive loading.
